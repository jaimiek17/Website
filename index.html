The part nobody names

Sound familiar?

  • The marriage ended, or it didn't, and you're not sure which one is harder.
  • The kids left and the house got loud in a completely different way.
  • Your body changed and nobody warned you what that would do to your head.
  • The career you built stopped fitting somewhere around year twelve.
  • You did everything right and you still feel like a stranger in your own life.
  • You keep asking other people about things you already know the answer to.

None of that is a crisis, it's information.

Listen to how you talk about yourself. I'm still tired. I'm still stuck. I'm still in the same spot.

Still isn't because you haven't changed, because you have. Your life just hasn't caught up yet.

What's actually happening

There's a name for this

I call it the identity lag gap. Your identity changed first, and your everyday life hasn't caught up.

I see this every day, and I see it get explained backwards constantly. Most people talk about it like your life raced ahead and you're the one running behind, trying to catch up to a new job or a new title.

That's not what's happening to you. You moved first. Everything around you is what's behind.

  1. Old life, old identity

    Built for who you had to be at the time, and it made sense then.

  2. Life strips you back

    Heartbreak, loss, burnout, or simply outgrowing the woman who built the life you're living.

  3. Your identity changes first

    You already aren't her anymore, you just haven't said it out loud.

  4. The gap

    Your life hasn't caught up yet. This is the blah, and this is the part you can't name.

  5. You build from what's true now

    Not what was true at 28, what's true today.

  6. A life that matches you

    Same woman, different direction, built on purpose this time.

You didn't build the wrong life. You built the life you built, because that was the direction you went in. Then life happened, and that life stopped being the direction.

Not better, not worse, just different.

This isn't about impulsive change, it's about intentional rebuilding.

Who's telling you this

My own story

I didn't wake up one morning and think, I'm going to become this new woman. No. Life happened.

I'm 40, the youngest of three, and I was never the first one picked growing up. For most of my life I didn't know my voice, I didn't know my power, and I didn't know my strength.

Sober since December 5, 2021. Fired three times in twelve months. Single mom of two. I've rebuilt this business and myself more than once, and had a real financial scare doing it.

Life stripped me back, and what I found underneath is what I do now.

I'm not healed, and I don't think anyone is, fully. I'd be suspicious of me if I said otherwise. I'm living the identity lag right now, I just have more road behind me than you do.

Still in it, just further down the road than you are.

You were told to choose between being the woman everyone needs and being the woman you actually are. You can be both.
